# Valencia Test

> Testing water governance realism against despotism potential.

The Valencia Test evaluates whether governance of a water supply can realistically maintain a non-coercive localized structure or if it will collapse into despotism. The five criteria are:

1. Clear boundaries on who's in
2. Rules matched to local conditions
3. Monitoring by all users themselves
4. Graduated punishment: fine > expulsion
5. Cheap, fast conflict resolution method

## Luminara Application

### River Systems
Luminara primarily relies on two major river systems:

- **[[Estroveil]] River**: The primary Trans Luminaran waterway running between two mountain ranges. The river has massive cliffs at its mouth, preventing maritime access - a logistical challenge that led to the founding of [[Vaelora]] as a river port city.
- **Columbine River**: Reserved for future Ancien civilization analysis.

### Early Water Governance
Initially, trans women simply accessed the Estroveil directly for drinking water. As irrigation became necessary, villages dug their own canals under engineering guidance from [[Vaelora]] (modern-day [[City of Luminaris]]).

### Local Distribution Model
Water is distributed through locally managed canals maintained by Vaeloran engineers based in the capital. Disputes are resolved through consensus-based assemblies where village spokespersons carry decisions from their citizen assemblies.

### Assembly Structure
Subregional water authority is determined by assemblies including representatives from each village and municipality. Each spokesperson carries consensus decisions from their individual citizen assemblies to a central meeting. Decisions only proceed when all parties reach alignment.

### Test Results

**1. Clear boundaries on who's in**: ✓ PASS - Village and municipal assemblies define clear community boundaries with citizen participation.

**2. Rules matched to local conditions**: ✓ PASS - Canal maintenance is guided by Vaeloran engineers in Luminaris while allowing local adaptation to terrain and needs.

**3. Monitoring by all users themselves**: ? NEED CLARIFICATION - Unclear how canal usage is monitored (communal oversight vs centralized management).

**4. Graduated punishment: fine > expulsion**: ? NEED CLARIFICATION - Actual punishment structure for water misuse not yet established.

**5. Cheap, fast conflict resolution method**: ✓ PASS - Consensus-based assembly system with spokespersons providing streamlined decision-making.

### Preliminary Assessment
Luminara's water governance system demonstrates strong potential for non-coercive management, particularly in its decentralized assembly structure and community-based conflict resolution. However, gaps remain in understanding the monitoring mechanisms and disciplinary procedures that would prevent abuse or misuse of shared water resources.
