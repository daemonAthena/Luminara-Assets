# History

A Refutation of the So-Called Stewarded Crown is a written work by [[Edmund Halverton]], Fellow of Political Rationalism, [[Royal Academy of Veyl]].

# Composition and analysis

The work holds that [[Luminara]] is a monarchy dressed in the language of consent, and that its Princess holds power rather than wields it. It states that authority is loaned, that councils govern and guilds labor freely, and that when conflict arises all eyes turn to a single figure whose word resolves deadlock.

The work argues that power which cannot be regularly, procedurally revoked is not stewardship but indulgence. It asks where the vote, the ledger, and the term limit are. It says feelings such as shame, affection, and trust are not safeguards but moods.

The work says that concentrating symbolic legitimacy in one person renders dissent emotionally treasonous. It says kings rise because systems fail, and that in [[Luminara]] the system is not law but personality. It concludes that the [[The Stewarded Crown|Stewarded Crown]] is an old tyranny anesthetized by beauty.
