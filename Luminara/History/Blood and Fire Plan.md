> A bold yet doomed strategy, the Blood and Fire Plan was conceived as a desperate measure by the Ancien government to quell the [[History of the Blooming War|Blooming War]].

The Blood and Fire Plan emerged as the Ancien government's response to its faltering invasions during the [[History of the Blooming War|Blooming War]]. Recognizing the need for a more potent and decisive approach, the plan aimed to leverage the rich manufacturing industry of [[Wayfire (Province)]] and the raw resources and experienced materials of [[Bloodmarch]] to create an elite unit of heavily armored mounted knights. These knights were to be known as the Draconic Inquisitors, named for their speed, defense, and destructive capabilities.

The Draconic Inquisitors were equipped with makeshift Greek-fire flamethrowers for arson and halberds, their mission being to commit pogroms on trans holdout settlements and end the rebels' supply disruptions. However, the logistical challenge of transporting these units overland, which crossed through the rebellion's areas of operation, was overcome by using sea travel. This innovative approach was both a stroke of genius and a harbinger of the plan's ultimate failure.

The name 'Blood and Fire' encapsulated the essence of the plan: a melding of the blood of [[Bloodmarch]] and the fire of [[Wayfire (Province)]] to create a devastating force. Yet, the plan was narrowly foiled by a piracy initiative bolstered by the rebellion's conquest of [[Greatmill]]. The [[Aurelian Intelligence Agency]] had picked up on the Ancien's plans, and in response, Luminara privateered the Grey Gulf using stolen ships from Greatmill to cut off Ancien's water supply lines. This move effectively strangled the logistical lifeline of the Blood and Fire Plan, dooming it to failure before it could be fully implemented.

The Blood and Fire Plan remains a testament to the ingenuity and desperation of the Ancien government during the [[History of the Blooming War|Blooming War]]. Its failure, however, serves as a cautionary tale of the pitfalls of overreliance on military might and the importance of strategic foresight.
